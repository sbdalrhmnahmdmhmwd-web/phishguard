How it works

Three steps from a suspicious link to a clear answer.

1. Break it apart

The URL is split into scheme, subdomain, main domain, ending and path. The main domain is what really decides who owns the site.

2. Run the checks

Over a dozen rules look for tricks like typosquatting, raw IPs, punycode, hidden @ symbols, risky endings and pressure words.

3. Score and explain

Each red flag adds points. The total gives a verdict, and each flag comes with a plain explanation.

Stay safe

Habits that beat most phishing attempts.

Read the domain right to left

Look at what comes just before the first single slash. That's who you're talking to.

Don't trust the padlock alone

HTTPS only means the connection is encrypted. Phishing sites can have it too.

Go to the site yourself

If a message says your account has a problem, open the app or type the address by hand.

PhishGuard is a student project. It's rule-based, so a "safe" result is not a guarantee. Use your own judgment too.
